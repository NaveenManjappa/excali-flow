Excaliflow guide

From a blank canvas to a flow story

This guide builds one small diagram from start to finish: a checkout flow. First you draw it and make the data move. Then you turn it into a story that you can present step by step or record as a video.

Part 1

Build the flow

A flow is a set of shapes joined by arrows. An arrow must be attached to a shape at both ends. Attached arrows are what the animation and the story follow, so the steps below create them for you.

1Add the first node

Double-click an empty spot on the canvas. A box appears with its label ready to edit. Type Web App and press Esc.

You can also pick a shape from the tool dock at the bottom and drag it out: 2 rectangle, 3 diamond, 4 ellipse. To rename any shape later, double-click it.

Hand tool first. Excaliflow opens with the Hand tool, so dragging the canvas pans it. Double-click and Tab work with any tool. To select, move or connect shapes by dragging (steps 3 and 4), switch to the Select tool with V or 1.

2Chain the next nodes with Tab

With Web App selected, press Tab. Excaliflow adds a new node of the same shape to the right, draws an attached arrow to it and opens its label. Type API Gateway.

Keep going: while you are still typing a label, Tab saves it and chains the next node straight away. Three Tabs give you a whole row of nodes without touching the mouse.

For the decision, select API Gateway, press Tab, then click the ◇ button in the floating toolbar above the selection to turn the new box into a diamond. Label it Payment OK?

3Branch with the + ports

Tab always chains to the right. To branch, hover over or select a shape and four small + ports appear on its sides. Each one does a different thing depending on how you use it:

  • Click a port to chain a new node in that direction.
  • Drag from a port and drop on empty canvas to create a connected node right there.
  • Drag from a port and drop on another shape to connect the two with an arrow.

To finish the example, add Retry Payment below the decision, then branch Order Service into Email and Warehouse.

Arrow tool. You can also draw arrows by hand with A or 5. Start the drag on one shape and release on another so both ends attach. An arrow that starts or ends on empty canvas isn't part of the flow.

4Shape the arrows

Select an arrow to change its route. The Straight / Curved / Elbow buttons are in the floating toolbar and in the properties panel (⌘\).

  • Drag an arrow's end to slide it around the shape's edge, or drop it on a different shape to reconnect it.
  • ⇄ in the toolbar reverses the arrow's direction.
  • Double-click a curved arrow to straighten it again.

5Make the data flow

The Flow: ON pill in the top-right turns the animation on and off. Excaliflow works out the order of your flow by following the arrows from the shapes that have no incoming arrow. Arrows further along that order start their particles later, so the flow plays out from start to finish.

Choose how the particles look in the Flow Animation section of the properties panel:

ModeLooks like
✦ AuroraA glowing trail that shifts from warm to cool colours. This is the default and suits tutorials.
☄ CometsBright heads with fading tails.
◎ PulsesRings that travel along the arrow.
⚡ NeonAn electric glow along the shaft.
⋯ StreamMarching dashes.
⊘ NoneNo ongoing animation. Pulse bursts still play.

Speed is 0.6x (the default), 1x or 1.8x. To fire a one-off burst from a node, select it and click ⚡ Pulse in the floating toolbar. The burst follows every outgoing arrow.

Part 2

Tell it as a story

A flow story walks through your diagram one step at a time. Each step has a caption and a set of spotlighted shapes. When you present, the camera glides to those shapes, the rest of the diagram dims, and pulses fire from the spotlighted nodes. The story is saved in the canvas file.

6Open the Story strip

Click Story in the top bar. The Story strip opens at the bottom of the screen:

7Draft steps with Auto

Click Auto. Excaliflow drafts a story from the flow order: an overview step called The big picture, then one step per layer (see the layer diagram in step 5). Each layer step spotlights the layer's shapes and the arrows coming into them, and uses their labels as the caption.

For the example, that gives the six steps shown above. Now rewrite the captions so they tell the story. For example:

  1. Here's how a checkout travels through our system.
  2. The customer clicks Pay in the web app.
  3. The gateway checks the session and forwards the request.
  4. The payment provider approves or declines it.
  5. Approved orders are created. Declined ones get a retry page.
  6. The customer gets an email and the warehouse starts packing.
Auto replaces the whole story. Click it on a fresh story, or when you want to start over. It also skips shapes that have no attached arrows.

8Add and edit steps by hand

To spotlight exactly what you want, select the shapes (shift-click, or drag a box around them) and click + Step. The new step goes at the end of the story and takes the first selected label as its caption. With nothing selected, + Step adds an overview step.

  • Edit a caption by typing in its chip. Captions can be up to 280 characters.
  • Remove a step with its ×.
  • Change the order by removing steps and adding them again. New steps always go at the end, so build the story in the order you'll present it.
  • Story edits aren't part of undo (⌘Z), but they are saved with the canvas.

9Present it

Click ▶ Play or press Shift+P anywhere. Excaliflow hides the panels, frames the first step and shows its caption at the bottom.

ToDo this
Go to the next step→, Space, or click anywhere
Go back a step←
Stop presentingEsc, or ✕ in the caption bar. Going past the last step also stops.
Start from a given stepClick that step's number in the Story strip

When you stop, the camera returns to where you were before. If you press Play on a canvas that has no story yet, Excaliflow runs Auto first so you always get a walkthrough.

10Record a video

Click ● Rec to export the story as a 1080p WebM video, with about 2.6 seconds per step. The camera moves, dimming, pulses and captions are all included, so you can drop the video into a slide deck or a pull request. Recording needs a browser that supports MediaRecorder, such as Chrome, Edge or Firefox.

To record the ongoing animation without the story, use ⋯ → Record 60fps Video in the top bar instead.

When something doesn't work

No particles move along an arrow.
Check that Flow: ON is on and the mode isn't ⊘ None. Then check that both ends of the arrow are attached: drag each end onto its shape until the shape highlights.
Auto leaves out a node.
Auto only includes shapes that have an attached arrow. Connect the node to the flow, then click Auto again.
"Draw a few connected shapes first."
Play and Rec need at least one shape. Draw some connected nodes first.
An arrow isn't highlighted in my step.
An arrow only lights up when the shapes at both of its ends are in the step, or when you selected the arrow itself before clicking + Step.
Undo didn't bring back a deleted step.
Story edits aren't in the undo history. Add the step again with + Step.
I deleted a shape that a step used.
The step skips missing shapes. If none are left, the step becomes an overview of the whole diagram.
